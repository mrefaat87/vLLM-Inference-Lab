Production Inference Platform on EKS
Phase 1 — Queue-Based Inference on EKS Phase 1

The EKS + Karpenter foundation: a queue-based inference load test measured at N=1, N=5, and N=15 concurrent requests, establishing queue wait time as the core scaling signal.

Breaks down per-request vs aggregate throughput and compares direct vLLM (Stage 2) against the queue-based architecture.
Phase 2 — Observability + GPU Autoscaling Phase 2

Adds the observability stack — Prometheus, Grafana, and DCGM — and uses it to watch GPU autoscaling in action.

Grafana dashboards captured under sustained load, queue saturation at N=15 on one worker, and the scale-up from 1 to 2 workers with the resulting queue-wait improvement.
Phase 3 — Admission Control: Should a Worker Decide Its Own Limits? Phase 3

Six rounds (A–F) of admission-control experiments: gating on memory bandwidth, GPU compute, both compounded, and KV cache; plus adaptive vs static strategies on 7B and 14B models.

Concludes with when each strategy wins, infrastructure lessons, and a layered-defense model for what to do when load exceeds capacity. The complete, consolidated Phase 3 results.
Phase 4 — Scaling Policies: When Queue Depth Isn't Enough Phase 4

Compares four autoscaling policies across a three-act workload — normal load, KV-cache stress, and burst — showing where composite KV-cache triggers beat queue-depth-only scaling by ~4x in throughput.

Covers the cost trade-off and the takeaway that SLA compliance is a capacity problem, not a policy problem.
Phase 4 — Cold Start: Why Yours Is Slower Than You Think Phase 4 · Blog

A narrative walkthrough of cutting GPU cold start from 590s to 212s, attacking it in three stages: image delivery, model-weight loading, and engine initialization.

Findings on EBS Fast Snapshot Restore as a warm-pool analog, why network locality and the RunAI Streamer underperformed, the cost of pre-baking images into the AMI, and a final decision tree.
Phase 4.5 — NVIDIA Dynamo Hands-On Spike Phase 4.5 · Spike

An exploratory spike on NVIDIA Dynamo for disaggregated inference. Headline finding: prefill/decode disaggregation regresses TTFT without RDMA, while KV-aware routing gives a modest p99 win on bursty traffic.

Documents the operator quirks that consumed the most time — namespace mismatches, the NIXL connector flag, and stale ReplicaSet pile-up.
